Common fallacies

controversy

Engineers and inventors have an unfortunate tendency of getting entrenched in controversies. It is certainly wonderful to be able to say "I told you so", and to feel more clever than the rest. I am no stranger to this kind of behaviour. However, I do try to stay polite and go the extra mile to convince others within their own frame of reference, instead of insisting on a "better" way of seeing things.

  There are two levels of controversy. The one is about pet design features. The other is of a more basic nature.

contradictions that aren't

The first items in the list are quite basic in nature. These are the ones that cause the most heated, and the most unnecessary arguments, viz. the supposed difference between Newton and Bernoulli including the "equal path" fallacy, and the infamous "downwind turn" syndrome.

flawed design ideas

The rest of the menu gives discussions of what I consider to be erroneous aircraft design ideas, like the canard and to a lesser degree, the T‑tail. I also tackle an almost universal, but flawed interpretation of the wing aspect ratio, which has led many people who should have known better, astray in their designs.
