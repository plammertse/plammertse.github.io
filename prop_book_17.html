17   The blade shape for optimized viscous loss

17.1   The blade shape varies with the drag ratio

Figure 17.1 gives the shape of the optimal induction distribution for our standard lightplane example of T ^  = 0.1, for viscous friction values of ε = 0 and ε = 0.02. The zero-friction case of figure 11.1 is shown in red.

There is a cross-over point at 0.7 R, and this is easier understood by collecting the terms of (16.12) differently. We separate the terms in T ^  from those in ε :

b  ( x ) = T ^ κ φ . cos 2 φ + ε . ( ⅔ . X . cos 2 φ κ φ   −   x ) (17.1)

In this formulation, the left hand term provides all the thrust. It is identical to the zero friction, rotationally optimal distribution of (11.11). This part of the induction is directly proportional to T ^ , and it does not depend on ε.

  The bracketed term on the right hand side is the viscous correction. In turn, it does not affect the thrust at all. This part of the viscously optimal induction is directly proportional to ε, and it vanishes for ε = 0. This term will always cross from positive to negative at app­rox­imately the following radius :

x = ⅔ . cos 2 φ κ φ . X ≅ ⅔ .  X (17.2)

The crossover is located at just over two thirds of the radius, near 0.7 R. This makes a lot of sense, since the net thrust of the cor­rect­ion term is zero, and in this way the cross-over location is almost exactly halfway the total disk area, since ( 0.7 ) 2 = 0.49.

The tip value of the distribution is :

b  ( X ) = T ^ κ φ . cos 2 φ tip + ε . ( ⅔ . X . cos 2 φ tip κ φ   −   X ) (17.3)

For large values of X, this is well approximated by :

b  ( X ) ≅ T ^   −   ⅓ . ε . X (17.4)

The tip chord vanishes when ε . X exceeds 3 T ^ , and this will give the blade a sharp point. More generally, ε . X ⁄ T ^ is the characteristic shape factor. It describes the balance between the two terms in (17.1).

  The correction is most pronounced for lightly loaded propellers of high tip speed ratio, combined with high values of ε. But even in our standard lightplane example, the change in shape of the thrust dis­trib­ution is quite apparent.
