Preface

purpose of the book

This text is a basic introduction to classical propeller theory. It offers the reader an intuitive grasp of the most im­portant concepts. This allows sensible preliminary designs, well informed sanity checks on existing design proposals, and parameter studies from very basic data.

  No prior knowledge is assumed except for high school physics, including a basic understanding of Newton's law and the concept of kinetic energy.

  A running example for a typical lightplane serves as a guide to the order of magnitude of various effects.

history

Historically, there seems to be a fresh attempt at writing an introductory text to propeller theory every few decades. The list of references gives the classical examples from the 1930's onward, through the 1950's and into the 1980's. They all cover the same basic ground as Glauert's 1935 classic synthesis in Durand, and the present text is no exception. However, there are two significant modernizations :

-   a novel, exact viscous optimization.

-   and a solid, simple lifting line code.

viscous optimization

This text takes Glauert's optimization of the viscous drag to its logical conclusion. I believe this material is new.

  This optimization by itself gives only a very minor increase in the efficiency of the propeller. But it is not just about dotting the i's in the theory. Somewhat unexpectedly, the viscous optimization gives a very noticeable modification to the blade shape, which may have some useful side benefits.

  The viscous derivation also helps to get a better intuition of the balance between viscous and momentum loss, and on the optimal value for the tip speed ratio.

simple lifting line code

The text gives an exact, yet extremely simple lifting line code based on Lerbs's 1952 version of Goldstein's exact vortex solution. With lap­tops on every desk, this code is both easier and more exact than Prandtl's classical approximation for the tip loss.

limitations

The calculations in this text are based on a desired thrust, not on an available engine torque or power. Design for given torque or power can be solved by simple iteration, starting with a thrust derived from the torque or power for an assumed efficiency of 100% or 90%, and converging quickly on the proper value.

  A more serious limitation is that this book does not contain an off-design computation for a fixed propeller design, optimal or otherwise, let alone for propellers of variable pitch. This may be added some day, but as it stands the book intends only to give insight into optimal propeller design for a single condition.

book or website

The Propeller Theory text on this website was originally written as a small book which was never published, but which does have an ISBN number, as shown on the frontispiece.

  While adapting the text to a website format, certain changes were made to notation and layout. Some effort has been spent to give the web pages a well formed print layout with headers and footers, but the printed pages will lack left-right gutters for binding and they do not have running page numbers.
