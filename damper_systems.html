damper systems

a single damper

We will start our exploration of state space analysis with a single mechanical damper. In a viscous damper, the damping force is proportional to the velocity :

F   =   − c  .  v (1)

We apply this to Newton's law for a floating mass :

F   =   m  .  a (2)

Here a is the acceleration, the change of the velocity with time. With Newton's dot notation for change with time, the equation of motion for a damped mass is :

v •   =   − c m  .  v (3)

This is, of course simply a scalar equation. But in the state space framework of (1), we can also write it as a single degree of freedom matrix equation ( leaving off the inputs for the moment ) :

[ v ] • =   [   -c/m   ] . [ v ] (4)

This equation says that v get smaller with time, at a rate equi­valent to its own size scaled by the factor −c / m.

  The slope of the graph of v against time is proportional to its own height, scaled by a constant factor. This is the graph of an exponential decay. Starting from an initial velocity vo, the solution is :

v   =   e  ( −c/m ) . t  .  vo (5)

As a check, the derivative of an exponential is the exponential itself multiplied by the factor on the variable. In this case the variable is t and the factor is ( −c / m ), and this confirms that (5) implements condition (3).
