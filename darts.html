Weathercock stability

an aircraft is two weathervanes

An aircraft is, among other things, a weathervane in two axes : one in the "short period" mode ( symmetric oscil­lation ) and one in the "Dutch roll" mode (asymmetric oscillation).

  This section will first discuss the weathervane, and then its close relative, the arrow or dart. This will lay the groundwork for the discussion of the "real" airplane modes.

weathervanes

We will start with the simple weathervane, or weathercock, as found on church steeples. Modern versions as in weather stations and on sailing yachts often take the shape of a simple fin on a pivoting rod, see figure 1.

Figure 1 :   Weathervane

Let us call the the distance between the fin and the pivot axis l f, the fin area S f, and the wind velocity V; and also, the angle between the fin and the un­disturbed wind θ ( Greek letter theta ).

  If θ is fixed, then the local so-called "angle of attack" AOA or α (  Greek letter alpha ) between the fin and the local undisturbed airstream and θ are the same. With the usual definitions for the lift coefficient of a wing, the rod will turn into wind under a torque M :

M = C L . α . ½   ρ   V 2 . S f . l f (1)

Suppose the moment of inertia of the rod-plus-vane about the axis of rotation is Ixx.

By Newton's law and with α = θ, the angular acceleration about the pivot axis will be :

d 2 θ d t 2 = M I xx = C L . ½   ρ   V 2 .   S f . l f I xx . θ (2)

In words, the second derivative for the angle θ with respect to time is directly proportional to the angle itself. This is the equation of a pendulum, or a linear oscillator, with frequency of oscillation ( squared, and in rad/s ) :

ω 2 = C L . ½   ρ   V 2 .   S f . l f I xx (3)

Note that this particular ω is not the angular rate of rotation of the vane. We will call that angular rate q later.

  The oscillation frequency will go up with the wind speed, and with the square root of the fin area S f. The jury is out on the arm length l f, because the moment of inertia Ixx will often increase with the square of that length.
